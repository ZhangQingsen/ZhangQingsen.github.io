Final Project Guidelines

CS 580Q: Quantum Computing · Spring 2026

Final projects may take one of two forms: an implementation-oriented Code Project or a literature-based Survey Project. Both formats should demonstrate a coherent understanding of a quantum-computing topic and communicate meaningful conclusions.

Code Project

Overview

The Code Project addresses a quantum-computing problem through a complete workflow: problem definition, method implementation, experimental design, result analysis, and conclusion. The project should demonstrate understanding of the selected method and provide meaningful interpretation of the experimental outcomes.

Topic Selection

Topics may come from quantum computing or related interdisciplinary areas, including quantum algorithms, quantum machine learning, quantum optimization, quantum simulation, quantum error correction, quantum security, and quantum hardware or noise models. Projects based on the Deloitte Quantum Sustainability Challenge are also acceptable.

A suitable topic should include:

  • A method or approach that can be implemented;
  • Experiments that can be analyzed.

Recommended Structure

  1. Problem Definition
    Define the core problem, its significance or application context, and, where appropriate, relevant classical baselines.
  2. Method and Implementation
    Describe the quantum method used—for example, variational quantum circuits, QAOA, quantum kernel methods, or hybrid classical–quantum pipelines—and relevant implementation details.
  3. Experiments
    Specify parameters, datasets, simulator or hardware environments, and comparative experiments. Experiments should be reproducible.
  4. Results and Analysis
    Present results with figures or tables where appropriate, interpret the findings, and discuss limitations or uncertainty.
  5. Conclusion and Future Work
    Summarize the work, identify possible improvements, and discuss the potential advantages and limitations of the quantum approach.

Deliverables

  • Written Report: clear structure and appropriate citations.
  • Final Presentation: problem overview, method and implementation, experiments and results, key insights, and future directions.

Survey Project

Overview

The Survey Project reviews and synthesizes representative research on a quantum-computing topic. The final survey should develop a structured understanding of the topic, its key approaches, and insights drawn from the literature.

Topic Selection

Topics may include quantum machine learning, algorithms, cryptography, error correction, simulation, security, hardware, or noise models. The topic may be broad or focused, provided that sufficient literature exists to support a meaningful survey.

Recommended Structure

  1. Problem Definition
    Introduce the problem, its broader significance, limitations of classical approaches, and possible advantages of quantum computing.
  2. Representative Approaches
    Select and synthesize key papers, focusing on their central ideas, contributions, and relevance rather than summarizing papers one by one.
  3. Method Categorization
    Organize methods into meaningful categories—for example, variational circuits, quantum kernel methods, quantum annealing, or hybrid approaches.
  4. Comparison
    Compare strengths, limitations, scalability, complexity, noise sensitivity, classical alternatives, and practical feasibility.
  5. Discussion and Future Directions
    Identify gaps, open questions, potential improvements, and promising directions for future work.

Deliverables

  • Written Report: clear structure and appropriate citations.
  • Final Presentation: topic overview, method categorization, key insights, and future directions.
