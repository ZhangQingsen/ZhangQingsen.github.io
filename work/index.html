01 · Doctoral Research

Wearable 3D Face Reconstruction

Binghamton University · 2024–Present · Advisor: Prof. Yincheng Jin

This project investigates how complete, template-aligned 3D facial geometry can be recovered from two miniature cameras mounted on the rims of ordinary eyewear. These cameras observe only fragmented and highly distorted facial regions, creating a reconstruction problem dominated by near-field perspective, self-occlusion, and missing information.

Data Collection

Designed and operated a dual-rim wearable capture system and collected more than 700,000 synchronized frame pairs from 17 Deaf ASL signers.

Pipeline Design

Developed a rim-to-canonical reconstruction pipeline using multi-scale view conditioning and rectified flow in template-aligned UV displacement space.

Evaluation

Implemented and trained the models in PyTorch, adapted comparison methods, and evaluated surface and facial-landmark reconstruction on participant-disjoint splits.

02 · Industrial Engineering

Machine Vision for Manufacturing

Wuxi Lead Intelligent Equipment Co., Ltd. · 2021

As a machine vision engineer, I developed vision software for engineer-to-order lithium-ion battery manufacturing equipment. Each machine configuration reflected different production processes, customer specifications, and mechanical layouts, so the vision system had to be adapted and validated in context rather than treated as a standalone algorithm.

Development

Developed C++ software with OpenCV for dimensional measurement, surface-defect inspection, electrode-tab positioning, and geometric calibration.

System Adaptation

Adapted inspection and measurement functions across machine configurations and customer requirements, while maintaining HALCON-based systems where applicable.

Commissioning

Spent approximately three months at customer manufacturing sites commissioning equipment, tuning vision systems, and modifying software as production issues emerged.

  • C++
  • OpenCV
  • HALCON
  • Machine Vision
  • Calibration
  • Industrial Automation
03 · Research Data

Egocentric Stereo ASL Hand Dataset

Binghamton University · Continuous sign-language data

This collaborative project develops egocentric stereo data for studying continuous American Sign Language, 3D signing-hand reconstruction, and manual-channel language understanding. Unlike the dual-rim facial capture system, this project uses a head-mounted stereo camera to observe hand motion during signing.

Collection

Contributed to collecting and organizing recordings from 25 Deaf native ASL signers under an approved research protocol.

Annotation

Took a primary role in processing and reviewing linguistic and 3D hand-pose annotations for continuous signing sequences.

Quality Assurance

Helped establish data-review and quality-control workflows for large-scale egocentric stereo video and associated annotations.
